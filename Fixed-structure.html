Oregon's Highest Peaks: A Journey From North to South

Map 1: Mt. Hood

Mt. Hood is the tallest mountain in the state of Oregon and features 12 glaciers, thousands of hiking trails, and is also home to the infamous Timberline Lodge from "The Shining"! Of the three peaks, Mt. Hood is the furthest North. It follows along the Cascade Range, and as we too follow these lines of volcanism to the south, two more iconic peaks cannot be ignored!

Map 2: Mt. Jefferson

Mt. Jefferson is the second tallest mountain in Oregon. It was named by Lewis and Clark for President Thomas Jefferson, and is roughly 60 miles east of Salem, Oregon. Interestingly, it is situated between Mt. Hood and South Sister in both its peak elevation height as the second tallest mountain in Oregon, and in its geographic region, where Mt. Jefferson is under the northern-justified Mt. Hood, but above South Sister which sits closer to the southern portion of the state.

Map 3: South Sister

South Sister is the third tallest mountain in Oregon and is considered a high-threat volcano. At South Sisters' summit you can find Teardrop pool, Oregon's highest lake! The furthest south of the three, South Sister differs from its more northern friends as it is part of a closely knit volcanic system. Mt. Hood and Mt. Jefferson are more isolated in their proximities and relations to other volcanoes, but South Sister likely shares related magma chambers with its very close neighbors like North Sister and Middle Sister.
